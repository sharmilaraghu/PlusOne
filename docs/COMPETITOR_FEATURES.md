# What each wedding planning product actually does

Researched 18 September 2026 for PlusOne. Every site below was read directly with Firecrawl;
the raw page text is kept in `docs/research/sites/` so any line here can be checked against
its source.

**How to read this.** Features marked **[seen]** were observed in a working product screen.
Features marked **[claimed]** come from the site's own marketing and could not be verified,
either because the product is behind a sign-in or because it has not launched. The
distinction matters: two of these four products are waitlists.

| Product | Who it is for | Status | What it is really selling |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Loverly](https://loverly.com/) | US couples | Live, free tools + marketplace | A media brand with free planning tools attached; money comes from vendor listings and the shop |
| [planning.wedding](https://planning.wedding/) | Couples and planners, worldwide | Live, free, no account needed | A complete free toolbox, unusually generous, with a vendor directory bolted on |
| [Wedd.ai](https://www.wedd.ai/) | Indian couples and their planners | Waitlist / early access | Guests, WhatsApp and photographs — *not* vendor booking |
| [Nupt.AI](https://nupt.ai/) | Busy US couples | Live, sign-in required | An AI that researches vendors on the open web and does tasks for you |

## 1. Loverly — loverly.com

A wedding magazine that grew a free planning suite. The tools are the hook; the vendor
marketplace, the shop and the editorial are the business.

### Planning tools
- **aiSLE Assistant™** — the couple forwards or uploads vendor proposals, receipts and
  inspiration, and it reads them and files each one into the right tool automatically
  (budget, vendor manager, vision board). **[claimed]** — the tool itself is behind a
  sign-in wall, so only the description on the homepage could be read.
- **Guest List Manager** — build the list, hold each guest's information, manage RSVPs
  per event. It collects guests' postal addresses by having guests fill them in
  themselves. **[seen, described in their FAQ]**
- **Vendor Manager** — search, add and keep every vendor's details in one place, and
  track payments made to each. **[seen]**
- **Budget Tracker** — every expense and vendor payment in one place. **[seen]**
- **Wedding Checklist** — a personalised task list and timeline, described as tailored to
  the couple's celebrations rather than a fixed template. **[seen]**
- **Vision Boards** — save real weddings and images into per-event boards. **[seen]**
- **Wedding Style Quiz** — a quiz that produces a style direction. **[claimed]**
- **Video series** — planning guidance in video form. **[claimed]**

### Marketplace and content
- **Vendor directory** by category (venues, photographers, florists, planners,
  videographers, caterers, cakes, hair and makeup, officiants, rentals) with a
  location search. **[seen]**
- **Curated "best of" vendor lists** per category and city. **[seen]**
- **Real wedding albums** — full editorial features on actual weddings. **[seen]**
- **The Wedding Shop** — products for the bride, the groom and accessories. **[seen]**
- **Planning articles** across eight categories, from proposals to honeymoons. **[seen]**

### Notable
- No account is needed to browse; tools require sign-up.
- Positions explicitly against Zola and The Knot, with a comparison table.
- **No outbound contact.** Loverly stores what the couple gives it; it does not write to
  vendors or collect quotes on the couple's behalf.

## 2. planning.wedding

The most complete free toolbox of the four, and the only one whose working app can be read
without an account — a project's pages are public to anyone with the link, which is how the
detail below was observed.

### The project shell
Every wedding is a "project" with a fixed set of sections. **[seen]**

- **Overview** — a dashboard of everything at once: wedding date, wedding party, checklist
  progress ("Total tasks: 103, Completed: 0"), budget, the big day, post-wedding, and both
  families' guest sides.
- **Guest List** — guests split by bride's side and groom's side.
- **Venues** and **All Vendors** — separate sections.
- **Website** — a wedding website builder inside the project.
- **Checklist**, **Budget**, **Event Itinerary**, **Seating Chart & Supplies**, **Notes**,
  **Post-Wedding** (photos), **Settings**.

### Each tool, in detail **[seen]**
- **Checklist** — 103 tasks out of the box, grouped into named stages ("Find Your Venues",
  "Start Planning Your Guest List", "Set A Budget", "Make Your Venue Choice", "Do Right by
  the Eyes of the Law", "Book Your Venue", "Gather Your Wedding Party", "Send Out Your
  Invitations"), each with its own sub-count. Counts down to the date ("4 months to go").
  Tasks are addable, removable and editable inline.
- **Budget** — categories and line items, each with an estimated and an actual cost,
  drag-and-drop reordering, and an "advanced mode" for more detail.
- **Event Itinerary / Day timeline** — a minute-by-minute default schedule for the day,
  from "8:00am Wake Up, Shower" through "1:15pm Photographer Arrives", "4:00pm I Do!",
  "5:35pm First Dance", to "10:30pm Grand Exit". Editable, and exportable as a PDF.
- **Vendor directory** — searchable by "Near" (local) or "Destination", covering wedding
  planners, entertainment, unique services and more. A couple can also **add a custom
  vendor** that is not in the directory to their own list.
- **Seating Chart & Supplies** — ceremony layout, seating chart maker, table cards, place
  cards and reception menus.
- **Wedding website** — built into the project rather than sold separately.
- **RSVP** collection, **online photo album**, **notes**, and **post-wedding** photos.

### Notable
- **No sign-up required** to start a project; login exists but is optional, and the app
  says so on every page ("Anonymous · Login | Register").
- Prints and PDFs are first-class — table cards, place cards, menus, the timeline.
- Aimed at couples *and* professional planners.
- **No AI anywhere**, and no outbound contact with vendors.

## 3. Wedd.ai — wedd.ai

India-first, and deliberately **not** a vendor-booking product. Its three pillars are the
guest list, guest communication over WhatsApp, and photographs. Currently a waitlist
("Be the first to experience Wedd.ai"), so everything below is **[claimed]**.

### Guest list
- **Collaborative contact import** — both families import their contacts into one list,
  with duplicates and incomplete entries resolved automatically.
- **AI grouping** — guests are sorted into meaningful groups on their own, such as
  "Delhi Friends" or "Work Colleagues".
- **Event-wise distribution** — groups are assigned to particular functions, with
  attendance tracked per function and reminders targeted per group.

### Guest communication
- **WhatsApp-integrated RSVPs** — invitations and RSVP reminders sent over WhatsApp,
  automatically, on the grounds that 90% of Indian wedding guest communication already
  happens there.
- **One-click data collection** — guest details from postal addresses to hotel check-in
  requirements.
- **Admin reminders** — real-time RSVP tracking, including chasing details collected offline.
- **Automated guest portal** — guests update their own information whenever they like.

### Photographs
- **QR upload** — guests scan a code and upload photos instantly at the event.
- **AI face recognition** — one face scan surfaces every photo a person appears in.
- **Full-quality uploads** — no compression, unlike WhatsApp or social platforms.
- **One gallery** replacing scattered WhatsApp groups, Drive folders and Dropbox links.

### A private social network
- A **private feed** for the whole family to follow the wedding, with no ads, no algorithm
  and no public Instagram page.

### Notable
- Two explicit audiences: **planners** ("ten weddings, one dashboard") and **couples**.
- The problem statement is unusually concrete: 95% of weddings run on spreadsheets, and
  wedding albums take around four months to arrive.
- Free for couples during early access.
- **No vendor search, no vendor outreach, no budget tool, no checklist.**

## 4. Nupt.AI — nupt.ai

The closest of the four to PlusOne: an AI that goes out and researches vendors rather than
listing the ones who paid to be listed. The product is behind a sign-in — `/onboarding`
redirects to a login offering email, Google, Facebook and Apple — so the feature list is
**[claimed]** from the marketing site.

### Vendor research — the core claim
- **Describe your vendors in plain words** rather than filtering a catalogue by one-word
  categories, with the pitch that the couple can be as specific as they like.
- **Searches the whole web**, explicitly contrasted with "other tools display vendors
  signed up with their platform".
- **Deep research on every vendor**, contrasted with "incomplete directories or
  surface-level data".
- **A personalised shortlist** rather than a page of irrelevant results.

### Everything else
- **Wedding website** — "we build the foundation, you add the finishing touches".
- **Seating chart** — an initial chart generated from the couple's preferences, then
  adjusted by hand.
- **Tailored plan** — "not a template", built around the couple's timeline, budget and what
  actually needs doing.
- **Bulk guest add** — the list comes together without manual entry.
- **An assistant that takes action** — "from creating tasks to managing guests, your
  assistant does more than just answer questions".
- **Multicultural weddings** — tea ceremonies, sangeets and other traditions can be
  incorporated.

### Notable
- Headline claim: **saves more than 500 hours**.
- Press logos (The New York Times, SheerLuxe) and a named investor.
- Founded by a bride who married in September 2023, after finding the popular sites
  frustrating.
- **Does not claim to email vendors or read their replies** — the assistant acts inside the
  couple's own plan.

## What this says for PlusOne

Three things stand out from reading all four.

1. **Nobody else does the outbound half.** Every one of these products stores what the
   couple already has, or shows them who exists. Loverly's AI files a proposal the couple
   already received; Nupt.AI finds vendors but stops before contact. Writing the emails,
   chasing silence and reading the replies back into quotes is genuinely unclaimed
   territory.
2. **The table-stakes gaps are the checklist and the wedding website.** Three of four have
   a checklist and two have a website builder; PlusOne has neither. The checklist is the
   cheaper of the two and the one couples expect first.
3. **planning.wedding sets the bar for generosity** — 103 starter tasks, a full day
   timeline, printable place cards, and no sign-up at all. Anything PlusOne charges for or
   gates should be worth more than that.
